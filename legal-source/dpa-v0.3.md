## 2. Definitions and interpretation

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Applicable Data Protection Law | The UK GDPR, Data Protection Act 2018, Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 where relevant, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 and amendments or replacement UK data-protection law applying to the Processing |
| Controller, Processor, Personal Data, Data Subject, Processing, Personal Data Breach and Supervisory Authority | The meanings given in Applicable Data Protection Law |
| Customer Data | Personal Data and other information submitted to, generated in or stored by the Service on the Controller's behalf |
| Main Agreement | The DREVORA Customer Terms & Conditions, order form, subscription agreement or other agreement governing the Service |
| Service | The DREVORA web application, PWA, Android application, APIs, storage, support and related functionality |
| Sub-processor | A third party engaged by DREVORA to Process Personal Data on behalf of the Controller |
| Restricted Transfer | A transfer requiring an approved safeguard under Applicable Data Protection Law |

2.2 References to writing include email and other durable electronic records.

2.3 If this DPA conflicts with the Main Agreement on Processing covered by this DPA, this DPA prevails to the extent of the conflict. Commercial liability caps remain governed by clause 19 and the Main Agreement.

## 3. Scope and processing details

3.1 This DPA applies only where DREVORA Processes Personal Data as Processor on behalf of the Controller.

3.2 The subject matter, duration, nature, purpose, Data Subjects and Personal Data are described in Schedule 1.

3.3 The Controller determines the purposes and essential means of Customer-controlled employment, workforce, compliance, vehicle and operational processing.

3.4 DREVORA may make day-to-day technical and organisational decisions required to operate, secure, maintain and improve the Service without changing the Controller's purpose or using Customer Data for unrelated purposes.

## 4. Dual roles and account-data classification

4.1 For Processing covered by this DPA, the Customer is Controller and DREVORA is Processor.

4.2 Worker names, work contact details, roles, organisation membership, profile data and authentication identifiers are normally processed on the Controller's behalf where they form part of the Customer's workforce account.

4.3 This DPA does not apply to information DREVORA processes as independent Controller for its own account administration, billing, direct support, security, fraud and abuse prevention, legal claims, insurance, accounting or statutory records. That processing is described in the DREVORA Privacy Policy.

4.4 If DREVORA determines the purpose and essential means of processing outside the Controller's instructions, DREVORA will act as Controller for that processing and comply with the obligations applicable to a Controller.

4.5 Passwords are handled by the authentication provider and are not stored by DREVORA in readable plain text.

## 5. Documented instructions

5.1 DREVORA shall Process Personal Data only on documented instructions from the Controller, including the Main Agreement, this DPA, Customer configuration, authorised in-app actions, support requests and other retained written directions.

5.2 The Controller instructs DREVORA to Process Personal Data as necessary to:

- provide, host, secure, back up, maintain and support the Service;
- authenticate Authorised Users and enforce organisation and role access;
- store, retrieve, organise, display, transmit, synchronise, export, correct, restrict and delete Customer Data through subscribed modules;
- support online and offline workflows;
- respond to authorised support requests;
- engage approved Sub-processors; and
- comply with Applicable Law.

5.3 If UK law requires Processing outside the Controller's instructions, DREVORA will inform the Controller before Processing unless the law prohibits notice.

5.4 DREVORA will notify the Controller if, in DREVORA's reasonable opinion, an instruction infringes Applicable Data Protection Law. DREVORA may suspend the affected Processing until the instruction is clarified or made lawful.

## 6. Confidentiality and authorised personnel

6.1 DREVORA shall ensure that persons authorised to Process Personal Data are bound by confidentiality or an appropriate statutory duty.

6.2 Access will be limited to persons who need it to operate, secure or support the Service, using least-privilege principles where reasonably practicable.

6.3 DREVORA remains responsible for compliance by its authorised personnel.

## 7. Security of Processing

7.1 Taking into account state of the art, implementation cost, nature, scope, context, purpose and risk, DREVORA shall maintain appropriate technical and organisational measures designed to protect Personal Data.

7.2 Schedule 2 sets out the current security baseline. DREVORA may improve or replace measures where the overall protection is not materially reduced.

7.3 The Controller is responsible for its own devices, networks, credentials, role configuration, exports, independent backups and removal of former users.

7.4 DREVORA is not a safety-critical, dispatch, payroll or emergency system. The Controller must maintain proportionate business-continuity procedures for outage, device failure and delayed synchronisation.

## 8. Personal Data Breaches

8.1 DREVORA shall notify the Controller without undue delay after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Personal Data Processed under this DPA and, where reasonably practicable, within 48 hours after confirming that the incident affects the Controller's Personal Data.

8.2 The notification will include, to the extent then known:

- the nature of the breach;
- affected categories and approximate numbers of Data Subjects and records;
- likely consequences;
- containment, investigation and remediation measures;
- a contact point; and
- information reasonably required for the Controller's notification duties.

8.3 Information may be provided in phases. Notification is not an admission of fault or liability.

8.4 The Controller decides whether to notify the ICO, affected individuals or another authority, except where DREVORA has an independent legal duty.

## 9. Data Subject rights

9.1 DREVORA shall provide reasonable technical and organisational assistance to help the Controller respond to Data Subject requests.

9.2 If DREVORA receives a request concerning Customer-controlled data, DREVORA will not respond substantively except on the Controller's instructions or where required by law. DREVORA may direct the person to the Controller and notify the Controller where reasonably practicable.

9.3 Assistance may include locating, exporting, correcting, restricting or deleting records where supported and lawfully instructed.

9.4 Requests concerning DREVORA-controlled account, direct support or security information will be handled by DREVORA under its Privacy Policy.

## 10. Assistance with compliance

10.1 Taking into account the nature of Processing and available information, DREVORA shall provide reasonable assistance with:

- security of Processing;
- breach assessment and notification;
- data protection impact assessments;
- prior consultation with the ICO or another authority; and
- demonstrating Article 28 compliance.

10.2 DREVORA may charge reasonable fees for substantial, repetitive or bespoke assistance not caused by DREVORA's breach, after advance notice where practicable.

## 11. Sub-processors

11.1 The Controller gives general written authorisation for the Sub-processors in Schedule 3 and for changes made under this clause.

11.2 DREVORA shall bind each Sub-processor by written terms providing an equivalent level of protection for the relevant Processing.

11.3 DREVORA remains responsible to the Controller for the performance of its Sub-processors' data-protection obligations to the extent required by law.

11.4 DREVORA will give reasonable advance notice of a material new Sub-processor, normally at least 14 calendar days where practicable. The Controller may object on reasonable data-protection grounds.

11.5 The parties will work in good faith to resolve a valid objection. If no solution is available, the Controller may stop using the affected feature or terminate the affected Service, with a pro-rata refund for an unused prepaid period relating to that affected Service where applicable.

11.6 Emergency changes required for security, continuity or law may be made on shorter notice, with notice as soon as reasonably practicable.

## 12. International transfers

12.1 DREVORA shall not make a Restricted Transfer except on documented instructions and with an appropriate safeguard.

12.2 Safeguards may include a UK adequacy regulation, the UK IDTA, the UK Addendum to approved EU Standard Contractual Clauses, binding corporate rules or another lawful mechanism.

12.3 The Controller authorises transfers made by approved Sub-processors where an appropriate safeguard is implemented.

12.4 DREVORA will provide reasonable information about relevant safeguards, subject to confidentiality and supplier restrictions.

## 13. Records, information and audits

13.1 DREVORA shall make available information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this DPA and applicable processor obligations.

13.2 The Controller may audit no more than once in 12 months, unless a breach, regulator request or reasonable evidence of material non-compliance justifies another audit.

13.3 Audits will normally begin with questionnaires, policies, summaries, certificates or remote evidence and must:

- use at least 30 days' notice, except for an urgent incident or regulator direction;
- occur during normal business hours;
- avoid unreasonable disruption;
- comply with confidentiality and security restrictions;
- avoid exposing another customer's data, source code or unnecessary vulnerability details; and
- be at the Controller's cost unless a material DREVORA breach is identified.

13.4 An independent assurance report or supplier evidence may satisfy an audit request where it reasonably addresses the concern.

## 14. Return, deletion and account closure

14.1 During the subscription, the Controller may use available export and deletion features or issue documented instructions.

14.2 Individual Admin or Worker account deletion may be requested inside DREVORA. When a deletion request is submitted, access for that user account is disabled immediately. Final deletion or anonymisation of that user's personal account data is completed within 30 days, unless the request is cancelled before the scheduled date by an organisation Admin or DREVORA support at admin@drevora.uk.

14.3 Operational, compliance and legal records may remain for applicable retention periods. Worker workforce records remain controlled by the Controller. For Personal Data processed on the Controller's behalf, DREVORA acts as Processor and the Customer organisation as Controller.

14.4 A sole Admin must appoint another Admin before deleting their own Admin account. Company closure is a separate process from individual account deletion.

14.5 At the end of Processing services, DREVORA shall, at the Controller's choice and subject to the Main Agreement, return or delete Personal Data processed on the Controller's behalf, unless UK law requires continued storage.

14.6 The Controller must request export before access ends or within an offered final export period.

14.7 Deletion from active systems may occur in stages. Protected backups may remain until the normal cycle expires, provided they are beyond ordinary use, protected and later deleted or overwritten.

14.8 DREVORA may retain limited independent-controller records for billing, security, fraud prevention, legal claims, compliance and evidence of instructions.

14.9 Schedule 4 describes the operational procedure.

## 15. Controller responsibilities

15.1 The Controller shall:

- comply with Applicable Data Protection Law;
- have a lawful basis and any required special-category condition;
- provide legally required privacy information to Workers and other Data Subjects;
- give lawful, documented and proportionate instructions;
- collect only necessary and relevant information;
- keep data and access permissions accurate;
- configure lawful retention and deletion;
- respond to Data Subject requests and regulator enquiries as Controller;
- ensure Authorised Users do not upload information they have no right to process;
- use medical or special-category functionality only where necessary and lawful; and
- maintain independent copies of critical payroll, safety, legal and regulatory records.

15.2 The Controller acknowledges that DREVORA does not independently verify working hours, holiday entitlement, roadworthiness, inspection accuracy, compliance decisions, uploaded documents or the Controller's lawful basis.

## 16. Processor responsibilities and unlawful instructions

16.1 DREVORA shall comply with direct processor obligations under Applicable Data Protection Law and this DPA.

16.2 DREVORA may refuse or suspend an instruction that would require unlawful Processing, materially compromise security, infringe another customer's rights or exceed the agreed Service. The parties will cooperate to find a lawful alternative.

16.3 DREVORA shall not sell Customer-controlled Personal Data or use it for advertising profiles.

16.4 DREVORA remains directly responsible where it acts outside or against lawful instructions, breaches processor-specific duties or determines a separate purpose and means of Processing.

## 17. Data Subject complaints, claims and regulatory matters

17.1 The Controller will lead complaints, rights requests and regulatory matters concerning Customer-controlled Worker and operational data.

17.2 DREVORA will reasonably assist and will preserve relevant records, provide available information and avoid making admissions on the Controller's behalf.

17.3 DREVORA will lead matters concerning DREVORA-controlled account administration, billing, direct support, security, abuse prevention and legal records.

17.4 Each party will notify the other without undue delay of a complaint, claim or regulator enquiry that materially concerns the other party's responsibilities.

17.5 Neither party may settle a joint matter in a way that admits fault by, imposes a non-financial obligation on, or materially prejudices the other party without prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.

## 18. Allocation of responsibility, contribution and indemnities

18.1 Data Subject and Supervisory Authority rights are not restricted by this DPA.

18.2 As between the parties, responsibility will be allocated according to causation and each party's breach, negligence, unlawful instruction, unauthorised Processing or failure to comply with direct legal duties.

18.3 The Controller shall indemnify DREVORA against third-party claims, reasonable defence costs and direct losses to the extent caused by:

- lack of a lawful basis or special-category condition;
- failure to provide required privacy information;
- excessive collection or unlawful retention;
- inaccurate or unlawful instructions;
- unauthorised disclosure or misuse by the Controller's Authorised Users;
- Customer Data uploaded without lawful authority; or
- failure to respond lawfully as Controller.

18.4 DREVORA remains responsible to the extent a claim or loss is caused by DREVORA's breach of processor-specific duties, Processing outside or against lawful instructions, unauthorised use, negligence or security failure.

18.5 If one party pays compensation for damage for which the other party is responsible, the paying party may seek contribution from the responsible party to the extent permitted by law and proportionate to responsibility.

18.6 Administrative fines and penalties are borne by the party against whom they are imposed, except to the extent recovery is legally permitted and reflects the other party's responsibility.

18.7 The indemnified party must give reasonable notice, permit participation in the defence and mitigate loss. No indemnity covers the indemnified party's own breach, negligence or unlawful conduct.

## 19. Liability and interaction with the Main Agreement

19.1 Nothing in this DPA excludes liability that cannot lawfully be excluded, limits a Data Subject's rights or restricts a Supervisory Authority's powers.

19.2 As between the parties, liability under this DPA is subject to the exclusions and caps in the Main Agreement, including the separate data-protection and security cap, except where applying a cap would be unlawful.

19.3 Each party remains responsible for its own compliance. Liability is not transferred merely because the other party is also involved in the Processing.

19.4 The commercial allocation in this DPA does not prevent a Data Subject from bringing a claim directly against a Controller or Processor where Applicable Data Protection Law permits.

## 20. Term and termination

20.1 This DPA begins on the Effective Date and continues while DREVORA Processes Personal Data on behalf of the Controller.

20.2 Confidentiality, security, deletion, audit cooperation, claims allocation and liability provisions continue for as long as relevant Personal Data is retained or a related claim remains possible.

## 21. General

21.1 Changes must be in writing and accepted by authorised representatives, except DREVORA may update Schedule 2 to improve security and Schedule 3 under the Sub-processor process.

21.2 If a provision is invalid, the remaining provisions continue and the parties will replace it with a lawful provision closest to its intended effect.

21.3 Failure to enforce a provision is not a waiver.

21.4 This DPA and the Main Agreement constitute the complete agreement for the Processing covered by this DPA.

## 22. Governing law and jurisdiction

22.1 This DPA is governed by the laws of England and Wales.

22.2 The courts of England and Wales have exclusive jurisdiction, except where Applicable Data Protection Law gives a Data Subject or Supervisory Authority rights in another forum.
